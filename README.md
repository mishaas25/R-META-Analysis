
**Image 1 — SMA 50/200 Crossover: Buy & Sell Signals**
Golden Cross (SMA50 crossing above SMA200) signals occurred in 2019 and again in early 2023, both preceding significant price rallies. Death Cross signals in 2018 and 2022 correctly anticipated downturns. The most recent signal in late 2025 is a sell/death cross, aligning with the current price correction.

**Image 2 — Cumulative Returns: SMA Crossover vs. Buy & Hold**
Buy & Hold significantly outperforms the SMA crossover strategy over the full period, growing $1 to ~$5.2 vs. ~$4.1 for the SMA strategy. The SMA strategy missed much of the 2023–2025 rally due to delayed entry signals, demonstrating the cost of being out of the market during strong uptrends.

**Image 3 — RSI (14-Day)**
RSI oscillates between overbought (>70) and oversold (<30) zones throughout the decade. Notable oversold dips occurred in 2018, 2020, and 2022 — all followed by recoveries. In 2025–2026, RSI has frequently touched overbought territory, consistent with the elevated price environment and recent pullback.

**Image 4 — MACD (12, 26, 9)**
The MACD line closely tracks the signal line, with crossovers generating frequent buy/sell signals. The most extreme MACD reading occurred in early 2022 (deeply negative), marking the crash's intensity. Post-2023 MACD stayed mostly positive, confirming the sustained bullish trend, though it has been weakening into 2026.

**Image 5 — Strategy Comparison: Cumulative Returns**
Across all strategies, Buy & Hold delivered the best long-term returns (~5.2×). The SMA 50/200 strategy came second (~4.1×), RSI reached ~2× but with long flat periods, and MACD performed worst (~1×), frequently whipsawing in and out of positions. For META specifically, passive holding proved superior to active technical strategies.

**Image 6 — Bollinger Bands (Last 1 Year)**
Price touched the lower band twice — once in April 2025 and again in early 2026 — both potential mean-reversion buy signals. The bands widened significantly during the mid-2025 rally and the early 2026 drawdown, indicating elevated volatility. Price is currently hugging the middle band around $610–$620, suggesting consolidation.

**Image 7 — Closing Price Over Time (2016–2026)**
META's closing price grew steadily from ~$110 in 2016 to a peak of ~$380 in 2021, before a sharp crash to ~$90 in late 2022. A strong recovery followed, with price surging past $700 by 2025, before settling around $610 in mid-2026.

**Image 8 — OHLC Prices**
Open, High, Low, and Close prices track very closely throughout the decade, indicating consistent daily price ranges with no extreme intraday volatility. The spread between High and Low widens noticeably during 2025–2026, reflecting increased short-term volatility at elevated price levels.

**Image 9 — Average Monthly Trading Volume**
Volume was highest during market stress periods — particularly mid-2018 and late 2022–early 2023 (the crash and recovery). Post-2024, volume has normalized to 10–20M shares/day, suggesting calmer institutional activity despite high prices.

**Image 10 — Yearly Average Closing Price**
Year-over-year averages show a near-continuous upward trajectory, with the sole exception being 2022 (avg. $180 vs. $321 in 2021). The acceleration post-2023 is dramatic — average price more than doubled from $261 (2023) to $670 (2025) in just two years.

**Image 11 — Daily Returns (%)**

**Image 12 — Distribution of Daily Returns**
The return distribution is approximately normal but slightly right-skewed, with the mean just above 0%. Fat tails are visible on both ends, particularly the left, confirming that extreme negative days occur more often than a pure normal distribution would predict.

**Image 13 — 50-Day & 200-Day Moving Averages**
The 50-day MA reacts quickly to price swings while the 200-day MA captures the long-term trend. Their convergence during the 2022 downturn and subsequent upward divergence in 2023–2024 marks the start of META's strongest bull run, with the 50-day MA well above the 200-day through 2025.

**Image 14 — Candlestick Chart (Last 90 Trading Days)**
The recent 90-day view (Feb–May 2026) shows a clear bearish phase from a February high near $740 down to a low around $525 in late March, driven by consecutive red candles. A partial recovery to ~$610–$620 follows, though momentum remains uncertain with mixed candle signals into May.

**Image 15 — Monthly Distribution of Daily Returns (Box Plot)**
Daily returns are broadly symmetric around zero across all months, with median returns near 0% in every month. January shows a slight positive bias while February and October carry larger outliers, suggesting event-driven volatility (earnings, macro events) rather than strong seasonal patterns.

**Image 16 — Price vs. Volume**
High volume periods (2018, 2022–2023) coincide with price uncertainty or crashes, while the 2024–2025 bull run happened on relatively moderate volume, suggesting price appreciation was driven by fundamentals and sentiment rather than frantic trading activity.

**Image 17 — Long-Term Price Trend (Linear)**
The linear trend line confirms META's strong secular uptrend from 2016 to 2026. While the 2022 crash deviated significantly below trend, the 2024–2025 surge pushed prices well above it. Current 2026 prices are converging back toward the trend line, near ~$550–$580.

**Image 18 — Additive Seasonal Decomposition**
The additive decomposition reveals a dominant upward trend component. The seasonal component oscillates by roughly ±$15 throughout the year with a consistent annual pattern. The residual component spikes sharply around 2022 and 2025, capturing non-seasonal shocks like earnings surprises or macro events.

**Image 19 — Multiplicative Seasonal Decomposition**
The multiplicative model scales seasonality relative to the price level. The seasonal multiplier ranges from ~0.96 to ~1.05, indicating the seasonal effect is proportionally small (±4–5%) relative to overall price. The random component dropped sharply to ~0.7 in 2022, capturing the severity of that year's crash relative to trend.

**Image 20 — STL Decomposition**
The STL (Seasonal-Trend decomposition using Loess) breaks META's price into three components. The trend component clearly captures the long-term growth curve — steady rise through 2021, collapse in 2022, and explosive recovery post-2023. The seasonal component oscillates consistently between ±$15, confirming a stable and repeating intra-year pattern. The remainder spikes most significantly around 2022 and 2025–2026, reflecting unexpected shocks not explained by trend or seasonality.

**Image 21 — Seasonal Component (Monthly)**
April is the strongest positive seasonal month (+~$16), followed by March and May, suggesting META historically tends to perform well in Q2. July is the weakest month (–$13), followed by December and November, pointing to consistent mid-summer and year-end softness. January also shows a slight negative bias despite being the start of the year. This pattern likely reflects earnings cycle timing and institutional behavior around quarterly reports.

**Image 22 — Original vs. First Differenced Close Price**
The original monthly close price (top) exhibits a clear non-stationary upward trend, confirming the series is not suitable for direct time-series modeling. After applying first differencing (bottom), the series becomes stationary — fluctuating around zero with no persistent trend — though volatility noticeably increases in 2022 and 2025–2026, reflecting the price crash and subsequent rally periods.

**Image 23 — ACF & PACF (Original and Differenced)**
The ACF of the original series decays very slowly and remains significant across all lags, a classic signature of a non-stationary time series with a unit root. The PACF cuts off sharply after lag 1, suggesting an AR(1)-like structure. After differencing, the ACF drops immediately to near-zero and the PACF shows only a few significant spikes, confirming the differenced series is stationary and supporting an ARIMA model with d=1.

**Image 24 — ARIMA(0,1,4) Residuals**
The residual plot from the fitted ARIMA(0,1,4) model shows errors centered around zero throughout most of the period, indicating a reasonable fit. However, residuals grow larger in magnitude post-2022, reflecting increased price volatility that the model struggles to fully capture. The ACF of residuals shows most lags fall within the confidence bands, and the residual histogram is approximately bell-shaped — both signs of a reasonably well-specified model with some limitation during high-volatility regimes.

**Image 25 — ARIMA Forecast: 12 Months (Base View)**
The ARIMA(0,1,4) model projects META's price to remain relatively flat around ~$580–$600 over the next 12 months. The confidence interval (shaded red) widens substantially as the forecast horizon extends, with the upper bound approaching ~$700 and the lower bound near ~$450. This wide cone of uncertainty reflects the model's honest acknowledgment of high price volatility and limited predictability beyond the near term.

**Image 26 — ARIMA Price Forecast: Next 12 Months (Detailed View)**
This cleaner ggplot version of the same ARIMA forecast shows the point estimate (red dots) stabilizing around ~$600 through early 2027. The widening pink confidence band again highlights growing uncertainty over time. Given recent price action consolidating in the $600–$620 range, the flat forecast aligns with a mean-reversion expectation following the 2024–2025 peak, though the model does not account for fundamental catalysts that could drive the next directional move.
